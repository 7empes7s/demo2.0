# Changelog

## Unreleased

- Fixed: the lit tab in the desktop header lost its icon (the icon took the global `.icon` sheet colour, ink on ink); the icons are now `.glyph` and follow the tab's text. On a desktop the file list now runs to the bottom of the window instead of stopping above an empty band.
- Easier to find your way without being shown. The sections are a bar along the bottom of a phone (This week, Files, Ideas, More), each an icon with its name, the current one lit in amber; on a desktop the same tabs sit in the header. "More" (`#more`) lists every other page with one plain line on what it is for (Check a claim, Official procedures, Votes, Send feedback, Settings). "On the agenda" and the sideways-scrolling tab row are gone from the top of every page. Lists are shorter: a file shows its place and a plain kind (Bill, Debate, Council point) instead of its number and official French label, a two-line title, and, in the full list, its step in words ("Step 2 of 4: Committee"). On This week each file has a calendar tile; today's files are lit in amber and say "Today". The paragraphs under each heading, the empty Panels box, the voting line and the empty "Elsewhere" box are gone; with no topics chosen there is one "Choose topics to see more" link, and "Everything else this week" is folded once a place is chosen. On a file's page a paragraph-long official title is set in plain type and folded after four lines ("Show the full title"), and Phases, Documents and History are folded under their name and count. Motion, in the Affichage verbs and off for reduced motion: today's date is stamped onto its tile after its sheet lands, the tab you press goes flat and the tab it lights pastes its amber square on, the tab bar stays put while the page under it peels, More's rows and an opened section paste in, and while the list loads blank sheets in the shape of the week wait under a marching cut line.
- Files read in the resident's language: titles, summaries, status, agenda steps, history lines, document names, committee and theme names come from the server's translations file (`data/translations.json`, the same for every device, saved for offline like the snapshot). "Translated automatically from French" with a "Show original" button sits under a file's title and on This week; one tap shows the French everywhere, another brings the translation back. French stays where a text has no translation yet, and each text carries the right `lang` for screen readers. The privacy trace now also switches language and the original toggle and still sees the same requests.
- "Your topics" left the This week page. A first visit opens on three welcome steps, one question each with big one-per-line choices and "Skip for now": the language you read best, where you live, and what you care about. Topics are now plain groups in the resident's language (Money and budget, Housing and building, Getting around, Nature and environment, Family, school and living together, Health and care, Work and business, Safety and justice, Culture, sport and leisure, How the state and the commune work, Other topics) that stand for the committee and theme names the Chamber and the city publish, including names the list adds later; a device that had saved published names keeps them through their groups. A Settings page (`#settings`, the gear in the header, "Change" or "Set up" on This week) holds language, appearance (like my phone, light, dark), where you live, the topic groups, the privacy line and "Forget my choices"; the header's language select and theme button moved there. This week keeps a four-line sheet: where you live, how many topics, a Change button and "Your choices stay on this device." Choices stay in `localStorage` (`d2.pulse.v1`: `home`, `groups`, `done`); the network sees the same requests whatever is chosen (tested on both paths).
- On a phone the screen now opens the way it does on a desktop: "On the agenda" and its section tabs (This week, All files, Check a claim, Official procedures, Ideas from residents, Votes, Send feedback) stay at the top of every page, and the tabs are one row that scrolls sideways under the thumb instead of stacking into rows of boxes. "All files" is a tab on phones only; on a wide screen the list is still beside the page. Desktop is unchanged.
- The commune's desk, in the served build when `healthz` says `desk: true`: "Official procedures" (`#procedures`) lists procedures by status with their own dated stage track, and opens each with its updates, published answers, linked ideas and official links; on a finished, stalled or cancelled one a resident says done, needs work or not done, with the counts. The Ideas page is backed by the desk when it is up (support, one per resident and never your own; a "Post an idea" form with language, title and text) and keeps the read-only Agora list otherwise. "Send feedback" (`#feedback`) needs no sign-in and answers with a lookup code shown big in mono, plus a field to look a message up by its code and read its status and answer; from a procedure or an idea the form opens with that subject filled in. "Votes" (`#votes`) shows the open questions, where a resident picks an option and can change it, and the published ones with a tally bar per option; counts stay hidden while a question is open. Posting, supporting, verdicts and ballots ask for the enrolment code inline ("Enter the code the commune gave you"); the token stays on the device. 429 reads as busy, 401 and 403 ask for the code again, 404 as gone, 5xx and outages as unavailable; answers are shape-checked before anything is shown, and no raw id appears anywhere (tested). All five languages; new terms in `VOCABULARY.md`.
- Dark mode is the wall with the lights off: a near-black wall, charcoal sheets, cream print, soft grey lines, and amber only for what is pressed, chosen or yours. The navy wall with paper sheets of the previous change is gone. Light mode is unchanged.
- Affichage is now the only look, and the flag is gone: `?look=affichage` and `d2.look` do nothing, and the old rounded look is removed. The sheets, marks, buttons, controls and motion moved from the flag's stylesheet into the tokens and into each component's own styles. Dark mode is redrawn: the wall turns navy at night and the sheets stay paper with ink on them, so every card, button and tag keeps the same contrast in both modes; what is written on the wall itself (titles, intros) is cream.
- The Affichage look, step three of three (only with the flag on): motion. New sheets are pasted on and settle (cards, files, entries, the Companion's answers), buttons press onto their backing, the old screen peels off and the next is pasted when you open a file or go back (a view transition where the browser has one; the header stays put), the quiz score flaps in like a split-flap board, and waiting is marching ants along the cut line instead of a fading pulse. All of it is off for readers who prefer reduced motion, and screen changes then switch on the spot. Without the flag nothing changes.
- The Affichage look, step two of three (`src/affichage.css`, only with the flag on): cards are stapled paper sheets with a 3 px ink border and a hard offset sheet behind them (amber for "Set up your week" and the current file, navy for "Next on the agenda"); files and options lift onto an amber sheet on hover; buttons sit on an ink backing and press down; tags, badges, reach and place marks become square inked labels in the display face; stage pips and bars are inked (done in ink, now in amber); verdicts, quotes, notices and the Companion chat are sheets too; selects, inputs and the segmented control get the same 3 px ink; every pill corner is squared. Without the flag nothing changes. Motion follows.
- The Affichage look, step one of three, behind a per-device flag: open the app with `?look=affichage` to turn it on (`?look=default` turns it off; the choice stays in `localStorage` as `d2.look` and is applied before the first paint). With it on, the app is paper on a wall: paper, ink, amber, navy and red tokens, no shadows, square corners, a dark mode; titles, labels and buttons in Big Shoulders Display, data in JetBrains Mono. Sheets, marks and motion follow. The fonts are added to the page for both builds. Without the flag nothing changes. The design and its pages are in `docs/design/`.
- "This week" (Pulse v1) is now where the app opens. A short setup asks where you live (Luxembourg City, Esch-sur-Alzette, or your canton for any other commune) and which topics you follow (the Esch themes and Chamber committees in the list), then shows this week's files (ISO week, Luxembourg time) under "Where you live", "Elsewhere, on your topics", "Panels" (explained as not started yet) and "Everything else this week", with "Your topic" and "Understood" marks, the weekly budget from the Charter (shown only, voting is not open) and how many files have nothing this week. Choices stay in `localStorage` (`d2.pulse.v1`) and a line says so; the list is built from the snapshot every device downloads, so no request depends on them (tested). On a phone, "All files" (`#files`) opens the full list, and a file's back button returns to where it was opened from; on a wide screen the list stays on the left. All five languages; new terms in `VOCABULARY.md`.
- "What does it actually say?" on each file (Arena v1): 3 to 5 quick questions on what the file's official text says, from `@democracy2/arena`, in both builds and with no Companion or network needed. After each answer: whether it matches the text, the quoted passage and a link to the official page or document, and whether the question was written by hand or made automatically from the official page. A progress bar, a score ring at the end, unlimited retakes with the answers in a new order each time, and an "Understood" mark on the file and in the list once every answer matched. Progress (attempts, best score, understood, per file) stays in `localStorage` and nothing is sent. All five languages; a note says when a question or its answers are shown in another language. New terms in `VOCABULARY.md`.
- Ideas from residents (`#ideas`, from a button under the list, served build only): the Agora queue, read only, in its ranked order. Each idea shows its reach (national, regional, local or small), title, text, support count ("Support count hidden for the first day" while Agora hides it) and age; the Charter version and raw ids sit under "Technical details", and pseudonyms are never shown (the server drops them and the app refuses a page that carries one). A notice always says posting and supporting open once secure sign-in is ready; there is no form or button for either. Empty state when no idea is posted yet, "unavailable" with a "Try again" button when Agora is down or answers anything invalid. Text missing in the reader's language falls back to French, German, Luxembourgish, English, then Portuguese, marked with its `lang`. All five languages; new terms in `VOCABULARY.md` (idea, support, reach, secure sign-in).
- "Argue the other side" lists the arguments each answer rests on, each with who made it and a link to its public source. A point the Companion wrote itself is marked as such in all five languages. Links open only for http(s) URLs. The app no longer asks for the documents' arguments when the server already says what it shows. The intro now says what the Companion actually draws on today: council groups' votes and official bodies' documents.
- Check a claim with Provenance, in the served build: on each file ("Heard something about it?", checked against that file) and on its own page (`#check`, from the "Check a claim" button under the list). It shows the grade (supported, unclear or contradicted by the documents), a plain reason, the passages and links to the official documents, and the ids under "Technical details". When no document mentions the claim it says so with no grade; when the checker is down it says it is unavailable, never a grade. Answers are checked against the grade schema in the browser too. All five languages. The single-file demo keeps the Companion's own claim check.

- Install and offline support in the served build: a web app manifest ("Civic Companion", navy and amber, standalone) with 192, 512 and maskable icons, and a service worker. The worker caches the app on install, fetches the snapshot from the network first and falls back to the saved copy, so the list opens offline. It never touches `/api/*`. Each build gets its own cache version and old versions are removed. A notice in all five languages says when you are offline. The single-file build has no worker and no manifest. The worker also uses the saved copy when the server answers 5xx or is slower than 4 seconds, only ever saves the app page itself as the offline app, never saves error or partial responses, and caches other built files on first use.
- Reads Docket snapshot/1 and /2. With Esch-sur-Alzette in the snapshot: each file names its place (Chamber or Esch-sur-Alzette), a place filter appears, council points show their reference, theme, last meeting and the council's vote (totals, per party, per councillor), and consultations show their summary, dates and phases. The stage track stays for Chamber files only.
- The place filter is a segmented control (All, Chamber, Esch-sur-Alzette) shown when the snapshot has 2 or more places. With every place shown, the list is grouped by place, Chamber first, so one council session's points don't bury the Chamber's files.
- A missing council vote reads "No vote recorded" in every language; consultations show their timing note; Luxembourgish dates read "2. Oktober 2026" instead of the browser's "2026 M10 2".
- Esch files open at `#esch.<id>`; Chamber files keep `#<number>`.

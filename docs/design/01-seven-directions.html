Seven looks that don't read as AI

The app is well built and the content is right. What gives it away is the look: it is the default look that every AI-made civic site has right now. Below are seven different worlds for the same "This week" screen, each one a live mock you can touch. Tap, hover, scroll. Pick one, mix two, or tell me what to push further.

Why it reads as AI today

  • Serif headline over a sans body on a dark navy ground, with one amber accent. This exact pairing is the 2025 default.
  • Rounded cards with a hairline border for every block, so nothing has hierarchy.
  • Pill buttons everywhere, including for navigation.
  • No material, no motion, no point of view. Nothing in it is Luxembourg's, or politics', or paper's.

01

Affichage

The public notice board, blown up. Thick black rules, flat primaries, hard offset shadows that move when you press them. Nothing is subtle and nothing hides.

Politics is loud and public. A notice board on a commune wall is honest design: black ink, one or two spot colours, big numbers. Neo-brutalism gives the app a physical, printed feel that no AI default has, and it photographs brilliantly in a tweet or a poster.

Palette

F3EFE4111111FFD23FE63B2E4F8DF0

Type

This weekArchivo Black for headings, Archivo for text. One family, two weights.

Motion & depth

  • Cards lift on hover and slam flat on press, like a physical button.
  • Page changes are hard cuts or a single wipe. No fades.
  • Week number flips like a split-flap board on load.

Light / dark

Dark mode inverts paper and ink (black ground, cream ink) and keeps the yellow. Still fully on brand.

02

Signal

Swiss International style, as used by passports, train timetables and ministries. White ground, black type, one red square, giant numerals, and a visible grid.

The most authoritative look there is, and the hardest to mistake for AI output, because AI leans decorative and this leans bare. Luxembourg's own red works as the single accent. The hero is the week number at 112px: the one fact every resident needs first.

Palette

FFFFFF000000E2001A666666

Type

41Schibsted Grotesk, 400 to 900. Numbers always tabular.

Motion & depth

  • Almost none. One thing moves per screen: the week numeral counts up on open.
  • Red underline draws left to right on hover.
  • No shadows, no radii, no blur. Depth is grid and rule weight.

Light / dark

Dark mode is black paper, white ink, same red. Equally strong.

03

Glass Chamber

Frosted glass panels floating in real depth over slow-moving coloured light. Tilt the phone, or move your mouse over it, and the stack parallaxes.

This is the direction that says "the newest technology in the world". It borrows from spatial interfaces (visionOS, the new iOS) rather than from web dashboards, so it reads as a product, not a page. Transparency is a nice metaphor for the mission too, if you want to lean on it.

Palette

0A0F243D5AFEFF9A3CFFCC70FF4ECD

Type

This weekSora, light and bold. Geometric, slightly futuristic, very legible on glass.

Motion & depth

  • Real 3D: cards sit at different Z depths; gyroscope or pointer tilts the whole stack.
  • Three light orbs drift behind the glass for the whole session.
  • Opening a file: the card expands and the rest recedes into blur.

Light / dark

Light mode is white frosted glass over pastel light. Needs care for contrast; doable.

Boldness highPhone fit very goodBuild medium, blur costs battery on old phonesRisk closest to a current trend, may date

04

Clay Commune

Soft 3D. Every element is a puffy, moulded object with an inner shadow, like a toy town of the commune. Rounded type, pastel clay colours, bouncy springs.

"Fun first" taken seriously. Democracy apps all look like tax forms; this one looks like something a 19-year-old would open on the bus. It also gives the Arena questions and the "understood" state a natural home: progress as a physical object you build. The playful look is a real bet, and it could be the one that gets Luxembourg talking.

Palette

E9E3F73B2F6BFFD166B9F0D8FF6B8AFF9F6E

Type

This weekFredoka for headings, Nunito bold for text. Both round, both friendly.

Motion & depth

  • Spring physics on every tap: things squash and bounce back.
  • The location pin bobs; the logo blob slowly breathes.
  • Possible real 3D: a low-poly clay model of the commune hall as the hero, rotating slowly (Three.js, one asset).

Light / dark

Dark mode is deep plum clay with the same pastel objects. Works well.

Boldness highPhone fit excellentBuild medium; 3D hero optionalRisk some will find it too cute for politics

05

Ledger

A verifiable public record, shown as one. Monospace everything, phosphor green and amber on near-black, faint scanlines, a content hash under every heading.

The whole system is about verifiability: Booth boards, Provenance quotes, Door credentials. This look makes that visible without explaining it. Every file shows its snapshot hash and whether its quotes were found. It is the only direction that is about the architecture, and it will speak to the crypto and civic-tech crowd you need as early adopters.

Palette

07090B9BE38FE9B9497FA68CF0F7EC

Type

This_weekJetBrains Mono only. Hierarchy comes from colour and size.

Motion & depth

  • Text types itself in on first open, then never again.
  • Blinking cursor on the prompt line; hover highlights a row like a selection.
  • Live: a soft green pulse when the snapshot is re-verified.

Light / dark

Light mode is paper-white with ink-black mono and the same green and amber. Think a printed receipt.

Boldness high, nichePhone fit good, denseBuild cheapRisk intimidating for the grandmother in Esch

06

Stamped

Civic paperwork made tactile. Kraft desk, cream forms, a typewriter face for data, a warm serif for titles, and real red ink stamps that slam on when a file is understood.

Government is paper, forms, stamps and seals. Making that physical and a bit theatrical is funny, warm and unmistakably human. The stamp is also a perfect reward object: "COMPRIS" slammed across a file you finished the questions on. Fully skeuomorphic, which nobody is doing right now, so it will stand out.

Palette

C9B58FF7F1E32B2622B8362B2A6B3F

Type

This weekFraunces for titles, Courier Prime for everything typed on the form.

Motion & depth

  • Stamps slam on with an overshoot; a tiny dust of ink noise masks each one.
  • Sheets lift and tilt a fraction when you touch them; perforated edges tear when you dismiss.
  • Paper grain is a tiny SVG noise, no images.

Light / dark

Dark mode is a green desk lamp on dark wood with the same cream sheets. Very atmospheric.

Boldness high, warmPhone fit very goodBuild medium, lots of detailRisk cute wears off if the content is dry

07

Kinetic

Motion-first. Oversized typography that enters word by word, a slow rotating colour mesh, a ticker of what is on the agenda, rows that invert when you touch them.

This is the "go crazy" direction. It treats the agenda like a festival line-up or a sports broadcast: urgent, big, alive. It fits a campaign moment, a launch, a young audience. It is the most distinctive and the most demanding to keep tasteful across hundreds of files.

Palette

0B0B0FF4F1FFFFB347FF3D8139E6C86B5BFF

Type

This weekSyne Extra Bold for display, DM Sans for text.

Motion & depth

  • Words rise in sequence on every screen; the mesh rotates for the whole session.
  • Ticker of the week's dates and bodies, always moving.
  • Rows flip to white on touch; the arrow travels. Transitions between screens are shared-element morphs.

Light / dark

Light mode is white with the same mesh at low opacity and black type. Still kinetic.

My recommendation

Affichage as the base, with the Stamped seals as the reward layer. It is the furthest from the AI default while staying readable for every resident, it is cheap to build with the tokens we already have, and the two share one world: public notices and official stamps. If you want the tech story front and centre instead, Glass Chamber is the one to pick, and it is the most spectacular on a phone.

Mixes that also work: Signal + Ledger (serious and verifiable), Clay + Kinetic (maximum fun). Tell me a number, or two, and I'll apply it to the live app behind a flag so you can compare on your phone.
